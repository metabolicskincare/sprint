# Start here

You're reading this one word at a time. That's the whole idea.

Somebody sent you a four thousand word document. You got as far as "in
today's fast paced world" and understood immediately that no human being sat
down and wrote this. But you still have to know what's in it, because there
might be one real sentence buried somewhere near the bottom.

That's what this is for.

## Why the words come to you

Normal reading is mostly eye movement. Your eyes jump along the line, stop
for a fraction of a second, then jump again. The jumps carry no information.
They're overhead, and they hold you at roughly two or three hundred words a
minute however clever you are.

Take the jumping away and that ceiling moves.

## The red letter

Look at the red letter. It hasn't moved once since you started reading.

That's the spot your eye lands on when it recognises a word, and it sits a
little left of the middle rather than dead centre. Every word slides itself
around so that letter stays exactly where it is. You're not tracking
anything. You're sitting still while the words come past.

## Going faster

Press the up arrow. Do it a few times. Each press adds twenty five words a
minute and the number along the bottom tells you where you've got to.

Three hundred will feel brisk today and slow by Friday. Most people settle
around four hundred after a week of it. Somewhere past six hundred you start
finishing documents before you've finished feeling smug about how fast
you're going, which is its own reward.

Down arrow when you overcook it. Everyone overcooks it.

## When you lose the thread

Hit the space bar. It stops.

Left arrow steps back ten words. Hold shift and press left to jump to the
beginning of the sentence you just mangled. Or grab the bar along the bottom
and drag yourself anywhere you like. Nothing's lost, the words are all still
in there.

## Two or three at a time

Bottom right, there are buttons marked one, two and three.

One word at a time is the pure version and it's where you should start. But
your eye can swallow a short phrase in a single glance, and taking in "in
the" as one thing really is quicker than reading "in" and then reading
"the". Try two once four hundred stops feeling fast.

Three is a lot. Three is for the document that opens with "let's dive in".

## The pauses

Commas get a slightly longer beat. Full stops get more than that, and the
end of a paragraph gets the longest one, which is why the sentences don't
run into each other at speed.

There's a checkbox to turn all that off if you'd rather have a metronome.

Either way the speed you set stays honest. The longer pauses get paid for by
shortening everything around them, so three hundred words a minute really
does mean three hundred words in a minute.

## That's everything

Drag any file onto this window to read it. Markdown, plain text or a PDF.
Press command and V to read whatever's sitting on your clipboard, which is
the quick route when someone pastes an entire essay into a message.

Now go and find out whether that document said anything.

It probably didn't. But you'll know in ninety seconds rather than ten
minutes, and getting those minutes back was the entire point.
